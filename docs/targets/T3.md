### The five to do first

1. **`davis-kahan-sin-theta`** (with its two small prerequisites `principal-angles-def` and `sylvester-separation-bound`). The single most-cited matrix-perturbation theorem in randomized NLA (gap-dependent subspace iteration and block Krylov, randomized PCA and spectral clustering, eigenvector accuracy of Nyström, leverage scores of approximate eigenspaces), it opens the new subspace-perturbation area and makes `wedin-sin-theta` a short corollary via the Hermitian dilation. Difficulty 2–3 per piece, all prerequisites present in NLAlib (orthonormal frames, projections, spectral theorem).
2. **`eigenvalue-perturbation-symmetric`** (Weyl for signed eigenvalues + Hoffman–Wielandt + the symmetric trace inequality). NLAlib proved these for singular values only; the eigenvalue versions are a day's work from `eigenvalues₀_add_le` and von Neumann, and are needed by Davis–Kahan's population-gap form, spectral-density/SLQ error, Ritz values and every 'eigenvalues of the sketched Gram matrix' argument.
3. **`ky-fan-maximum-principle`**. Difficulty 2 given interlacing and von Neumann; it is the gateway to the nuclear/Ky Fan norms (`ky-fan-norms-def`, which TARGETING §3 already wants and `nystrom-randomized` states its result in), Schur–Horn, and Lidskii.
4. **`loewner-heinz-real`**. Mathlib has Löwner–Heinz, inverse antitonicity and log monotonicity but only for complex C*-algebras; NLAlib's matrix layer is real. A transport lemma (difficulty 2) unlocks the inverse/square-root sandwiches used by every subspace-embedding ⇒ preconditioner argument (`sketch-and-precondition`, `nystrom-pcg`, `ose-def` equivalent forms, ridge leverage scores).
5. **`generalized-schur-complement-psd`**. Mathlib's Schur-complement PSD criterion needs an invertible pivot; Nyström and partial Cholesky have singular pivots. Closes the unformalised Schur clause of `nystrom-structural`, and is the step `rpcholesky` and `cholesky-decomposition` rest on.

Quick win alongside these: **`inverse-perturbation`** (difficulty 1; explicit Neumann/inverse-perturbation constants in `specNorm`), which feeds `linear-system-perturbation`, `pseudoinverse-perturbation` and the preconditioning consumers.

### `principal-angles-def` — Canonical (principal) angles between subspaces and sin Θ

*definition* · area `matrix-subspace-perturbation` (Subspace angles and sin θ theorems) · difficulty 2 · priority `now` · **Prove.**

For $Q,\hat Q\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}$ with orthonormal columns, the canonical angles $0\le\theta_1\le\dots\le\theta_k\le\pi/2$ are defined by $\cos\theta_i=\sigma_i(Q^\top\hat Q)$ (so $\|\sin\Theta\|=\sin\theta_k=\sqrt{1-\sigma_k(Q^\top\hat Q)^2}$), and $\sin\Theta(Q,\hat Q)=\operatorname{diag}(\sin\theta_i)$. Characterisations, with $Q_\perp$ an orthonormal complement of $Q$: $\|\sin\Theta\|=\|Q_\perp^\top\hat Q\|=\|(I-QQ^\top)\hat Q\|=\|QQ^\top-\hat Q\hat Q^\top\|$; $\|\sin\Theta\|_F^2=k-\|Q^\top\hat Q\|_F^2=\tfrac12\|QQ^\top-\hat Q\hat Q^\top\|_F^2$; $\Theta$ depends only on the ranges, is symmetric in $(Q,\hat Q)$; if $\theta_k<\pi/2$, $\|\tan\Theta\|=\|(Q_\perp^\top\hat Q)(Q^\top\hat Q)^{-1}\|$.

**Hypotheses.**
- $Q,\hat Q\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}$ with $Q^\top Q=\hat Q^\top\hat Q=I_k$
- $Q_\perp\in\mathbb R^{n\times(n-k)}$ with $[Q\ Q_\perp]$ orthogonal

**Conclusion.** $\|\sin\Theta\|=\|Q_\perp^\top\hat Q\|=\|(I-QQ^\top)\hat Q\|=\|QQ^\top-\hat Q\hat Q^\top\|$, $\|\sin\Theta\|_F^2=k-\|Q^\top\hat Q\|_F^2=\tfrac12\|QQ^\top-\hat Q\hat Q^\top\|_F^2$, and the invariance/symmetry properties

**Sources.** Stewart (`ss90`) §I.5 (Def I.5.3, Thm I.5.5); Golub (`gvl13`) §2.5.3 (Thm 2.5.1), §6.4.3; Bhatia (`bhatia97`) §VII.1

**Mathlib has.** `Submodule.starProjection` (Orthogonal projections onto submodules exist; angles between subspaces do not.); `LinearMap.singularValues`

**Mathlib lacks.** Principal angles

**NLAlib builds on.** `NLAlib.HasOrthonormalCols`, `exists_orthonormal_complement`, `residual`, `frobSq_residual_eq_sub`, `specNorm_mul_left_of_hasOrthonormalCols`, `singularValues`. Depends on: `svd`, `orthonormal-completion`, `projection-facts`, `orthonormal-columns-def`.

**Consumers.** davis-kahan-sin-theta, wedin-sin-theta, pseudoinverse-perturbation, subspace-iteration-expected, block-krylov-lra, random-start-power.

**Notes.** State with matrices (HasOrthonormalCols) rather than submodules, as every consumer manipulates frames; give the submodule-level invariance as a lemma. Define the angles through NLAlib.singularValues (Qᵀ Q̂) on Fin k indices and expose ‖sin Θ‖ as specNorm (residual Q Q̂) so the projections API applies directly. Consumers: davis-kahan-sin-theta, wedin-sin-theta, pseudoinverse-perturbation (projector part), subspace-iteration-expected and block-krylov-lra (gap-dependent tan Θ forms), random-start-power (tan θ of the start vector), hmt-9-1-spectral (Ω₂Ω₁† is a tan Θ), leverage-score approximation from approximate bases. ### `sylvester-separation-bound` — Separation bound for the Sylvester operator X ↦ LX − XM

*lemma* · area `matrix-subspace-perturbation` (Subspace angles and sin θ theorems) · difficulty 3 · priority `now` · **Prove.**

Let $L\in\mathbb R^{p\times p}$, $M\in\mathbb R^{q\times q}$ be symmetric and $X\in\mathbb R^{p\times q}$. (a) If $|\lambda_i(L)-\lambda_j(M)|\ge\delta>0$ for all $i,j$ then $\|LX-XM\|_F\ge\delta\|X\|_F$. (b) If the eigenvalues of $M$ lie in $[a,b]$ and those of $L$ lie outside $(a-\delta,b+\delta)$, $\delta>0$, then $\|LX-XM\|\ge\delta\|X\|$ (spectral norm; also Frobenius). The one-sided case $\lambda_{\max}(L)\le\alpha-\delta$, $\lambda_{\min}(M)\ge\alpha$ is the one used for top-$k$ subspaces.

**Hypotheses.**
- $L=L^\top\in\mathbb R^{p\times p}$, $M=M^\top\in\mathbb R^{q\times q}$, $X\in\mathbb R^{p\times q}$, $\delta>0$
- (a) $\min_{i,j}|\lambda_i(L)-\lambda_j(M)|\ge\delta$
- (b) $\sigma(M)\subseteq[a,b]$ and $\sigma(L)\cap(a-\delta,b+\delta)=\emptyset$

**Conclusion.** (a) $\delta\|X\|_F\le\|LX-XM\|_F$; (b) $\delta\|X\|\le\|LX-XM\|$ and $\delta\|X\|_F\le\|LX-XM\|_F$

**Sources.** Bhatia (`bhatia97`) §VII.2; Davis (`dk70`) §5–6; Stewart (`ss90`) §V.3

**Mathlib has.** `Matrix.IsHermitian.spectral_theorem`

**Mathlib lacks.** Sylvester separation

**NLAlib builds on.** `NLAlib.eq_eigenvectorUnitary_mul_diagonal_mul_transpose`, `frobSq_mul_left_of_orthonormal`, `specNorm_mul_left_of_hasOrthonormalCols`. Depends on: `svd`, `courant-fischer`, `quadform-def`.

**Consumers.** davis-kahan-sin-theta, wedin-sin-theta.

**Notes.** Engine of both sin θ theorems; keeping it separate lets Davis–Kahan and Wedin share it. (a) is entrywise after diagonalising L and M (difficulty 2). (b) in the spectral norm needs the interval/one-sided structure (with arbitrary interlaced spectra the best constant is π/2, Bhatia–Davis–McIntosh; do not state that). The one-sided case follows from a shift and a positivity argument. Labels in bhatia97 §VII.2 to verify. Consumers: davis-kahan-sin-theta, wedin-sin-theta, eigenvalue-residual-bounds (k = 1 case). Recommendation: prove.

### `davis-kahan-sin-theta` — Davis–Kahan sin Θ theorem for invariant subspaces of symmetric matrices

*theorem* · area `matrix-subspace-perturbation` (Subspace angles and sin θ theorems) · difficulty 3 · priority `now` · **Prove.**

$A,\hat A=A+E\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ symmetric. $Q_1\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}$ orthonormal with $AQ_1=Q_1\Lambda_1$; $[\hat Q_1\ \hat Q_2]$ orthogonal with $\hat A\hat Q_i=\hat Q_i\hat\Lambda_i$. If $\sigma(\Lambda_1)\subseteq[a,b]$ and $\sigma(\hat\Lambda_2)\cap(a-\delta,b+\delta)=\emptyset$ with $\delta>0$, then $$\|\sin\Theta(Q_1,\hat Q_1)\|=\|\hat Q_2^\top Q_1\|\le\frac{\|\hat Q_2^\top EQ_1\|}{\delta}\le\frac{\|EQ_1\|}{\delta}\le\frac{\|E\|}{\delta},$$ and the same with Frobenius norms. Top-$k$ corollary: with $Q_1,\hat Q_1$ top-$k$ eigenvectors, $\|\sin\Theta\|\le\|E\|/(\lambda_k(A)-\lambda_{k+1}(\hat A))$ when the denominator is positive. Population-gap form (Yu–Wang–Samworth): if $g=\lambda_k(A)-\lambda_{k+1}(A)>0$ then $\|\sin\Theta\|_F\le2\min(\sqrt k\|E\|,\|E\|_F)/g$ and $\|\sin\Theta\|\le2\|E\|/g$.

**Hypotheses.**
- $A=A^\top$, $E=E^\top\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$, $\hat A=A+E$
- $Q_1\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}$ orthonormal, $AQ_1=Q_1\Lambda_1$; $[\hat Q_1\ \hat Q_2]$ orthogonal, $\hat A\hat Q_2=\hat Q_2\hat\Lambda_2$
- $\delta>0$, $\sigma(\Lambda_1)\subseteq[a,b]$, $\sigma(\hat\Lambda_2)\cap(a-\delta,b+\delta)=\emptyset$
- Population-gap form: $Q_1,\hat Q_1$ top-$k$ eigenvectors, $\lambda_k(A)>\lambda_{k+1}(A)$

**Conclusion.** $\|\sin\Theta(Q_1,\hat Q_1)\|\le\|\hat Q_2^\top EQ_1\|/\delta\le\|E\|/\delta$ (also in $\|\cdot\|_F$); $\|\sin\Theta\|_F\le2\min(\sqrt k\|E\|,\|E\|_F)/(\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1})$

**Sources.** Davis (`dk70`) sin θ theorem (§6); Bhatia (`bhatia97`) Thm VII.3.1; Stewart (`ss90`) Thm V.3.6; Yu (`ywss15`) Thm 1

**Mathlib has.** nothing directly relevant.

**Mathlib lacks.** Davis–Kahan

**NLAlib builds on.** `NLAlib.HasOrthonormalCols`, projections API, Weyl for eigenvalues once eigenvalue-perturbation-symmetric lands. Depends on: `sylvester-separation-bound`, `principal-angles-def`, `eigenvalue-perturbation-symmetric`.

**Consumers.** wedin-sin-theta, subspace-iteration-expected, block-krylov-lra, random-start-power, leverage-scores, randomized PCA/spectral clustering.

**Notes.** Proof: Q̂₂ᵀ(ÂQ₁ − Q₁Λ₁) = Λ̂₂(Q̂₂ᵀQ₁) − (Q̂₂ᵀQ₁)Λ₁ and ÂQ₁ − Q₁Λ₁ = EQ₁, then sylvester-separation-bound. The population-gap form: if ‖E‖ ≥ g/2 the bound is trivial (sin ≤ 1), otherwise Weyl (eigenvalue-perturbation-symmetric) gives λ_k(A) − λ_{k+1}(Â) ≥ g/2. Consumers: wedin-sin-theta; gap-dependent analyses of subspace-iteration-expected and block-krylov-lra; randomized PCA / spectral clustering from sketches; Nyström eigenvector accuracy (nystrom-randomized follow-ups); leverage scores of approximate eigenspaces (leverage-scores); random-start-power's gap form. Recommendation: prove.

### `eigenvalue-perturbation-symmetric` — Weyl and Hoffman–Wielandt perturbation bounds for symmetric eigenvalues

*theorem* · area `matrix-spectral` (SVD, eigenvalues and perturbation) · difficulty 2 · priority `now` · **Prove; difficulty 2.**

For symmetric $A,E\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ with decreasing eigenvalues: (Weyl) $\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_n(E)\le\lambda_i(A+E)\le\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_1(E)$, hence $|\lambda_i(A+E)-\lambda_i(A)|\le\|E\|$; (trace inequality) $\operatorname{tr}(AB)\le\sum_i\lambda_i(A)\lambda_i(B)$ for symmetric $A,B$; (Hoffman–Wielandt) $$\sum_i\big(\lambda_i(A+E)-\lambda_i(A)\big)^2\le\|E\|_F^2.$$

**Hypotheses.**
- $A=A^\top$, $E=E^\top\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ (any `Fintype` index, eigenvalues via `eigenvalues₀`)

**Conclusion.** $\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_{\min}(E)\le\lambda_i(A+E)\le\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_{\max}(E)$; $|\lambda_i(A+E)-\lambda_i(A)|\le\|E\|$; $\operatorname{tr}(AB)\le\sum_i\lambda_i(A)\lambda_i(B)$; $\sum_i(\lambda_i(A+E)-\lambda_i(A))^2\le\|E\|_F^2$

**Sources.** Horn (`hj13`) Thm 4.3.1, Cor 4.3.15 (Weyl); Cor 6.3.8 (Hoffman–Wielandt); Bhatia (`bhatia97`) Cor III.2.6, Thm VI.4.1

**Mathlib has.** `Matrix.IsHermitian.eigenvalues₀`; `Matrix.IsHermitian.eigenvalues₀_antitone`; `Matrix.IsHermitian.trace_eq_sum_eigenvalues`

**Mathlib lacks.** Eigenvalue perturbation bounds

**NLAlib builds on.** `NLAlib.eigenvalues₀_add_le`, `eigenvalues₀_le_eigenvalues₀_of_posSemidef_sub`, `frobInner_le_sum_singularValues_mul`, `frobSq_eq_sum_eigenvalues_sq` (Estimation.HutchinsonTail). Depends on: `courant-fischer`, `von-neumann-trace`, `svd`.

**Consumers.** davis-kahan-sin-theta, slq-error, ritz-value-bounds, nystrom-randomized, sketch-and-precondition.

**Notes.** weyl-mirsky covers singular values only; the symmetric-eigenvalue versions (which keep signs) are missing although NLAlib.eigenvalues₀_add_le (Weyl i+j) and eigenvalues₀_le_eigenvalues₀_of_posSemidef_sub (Loewner monotonicity) are there. Consumers: davis-kahan-sin-theta (population-gap corollary), slq-error and spectral-density bounds (W₂ distance between spectra ≤ ‖E‖_F/√n), ritz-value-bounds, nystrom-structural/nystrom-randomized eigenvalue statements, matrix-chernoff-sampling consequences, sketch-and-precondition (eigenvalues of AᵀSᵀSA). ### `ky-fan-maximum-principle` — Ky Fan maximum principle for eigenvalue and singular-value sums

*theorem* · area `matrix-majorization` (Majorization, Ky Fan and unitarily invariant norms) · difficulty 2 · priority `now` · **Prove; a short corollary of interlacing and von Neumann.**

For symmetric $A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1\ge\dots\ge\lambda_n$ and $0\le k\le n$: $$\sum_{i=1}^k\lambda_i(A)=\max\{\operatorname{tr}(Q^\top AQ): Q\in\mathbb R^{n\times k},\ Q^\top Q=I_k\},$$ attained at any $k$ orthonormal top eigenvectors (dually, the bottom $k$ sum is the minimum). For $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$: $\sum_{i\le k}\sigma_i(A)=\max\{\operatorname{tr}(U^\top AV): U^\top U=V^\top V=I_k\}$. Corollary: $\sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i(A+B)\le\sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i(A)+\sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i(B)$, i.e. $\lambda(A+B)\prec\lambda(A)+\lambda(B)$, and $\sigma(A+B)\prec_w\sigma(A)+\sigma(B)$.

**Hypotheses.**
- $A=A^\top\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$, eigenvalues in decreasing order (`eigenvalues₀`)
- $Q\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}$ with orthonormal columns
- Singular-value form: $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$, $U\in\mathbb R^{m\times k}$, $V\in\mathbb R^{n\times k}$ with orthonormal columns, $k\le\min(m,n)$

**Conclusion.** $\operatorname{tr}(Q^\top AQ)\le\sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i(A)$ with equality for top eigenvectors; $\operatorname{tr}(U^\top AV)\le\sum_{i\le k}\sigma_i(A)$ with equality for top singular vectors

**Sources.** Fan (`fan49`) Thm 1; Horn (`hj13`) Cor 4.3.39; Horn (`hj91`) Thm 3.4.1

### `majorization-def` — Majorization and weak majorization of real vectors

*definition* · area `matrix-majorization` (Majorization, Ky Fan and unitarily invariant norms) · difficulty 3 · priority `next` · **Prove (definition + easy directions); assume the HLP (⇒) direction if needed early.**

**Hypotheses.**
- $x, y \in \mathbb{R}^n$ (index type `Fin n`, or any `Fintype` index)
- For the function characterisations: $\varphi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ convex (increasing and convex for $\prec_w$)

**Conclusion.** $x\prec y \iff \exists D \text{ doubly stochastic},\ x = Dy \iff \forall\varphi \text{ convex},\ \sum_i\varphi(x_i)\le\sum_i\varphi(y_i)$; and $x\prec_w y\iff\forall\varphi$ increasing convex, $\sum_i\varphi(x_i)\le\sum_i\varphi(y_i)$

**Sources.** Marshall (`mo11`) Def 1.A.1, 1.A.2; Thm 2.B.2 (HLP); Prop 4.B.1–4.B.2; Bhatia (`bhatia97`) §II.1–II.3

### `unitarily-invariant-norm-def` — Unitarily invariant norms: von Neumann's gauge characterisation and Fan dominance

*definition* · area `matrix-majorization` (Majorization, Ky Fan and unitarily invariant norms) · difficulty 4 · priority `later` · **Prove the definition; assume Fan dominance (⇐).**

A norm on $\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ is orthogonally (unitarily) invariant if $\|UAV\|=\|A\|$ for all orthogonal $U,V$. (von Neumann) These are exactly $\|A\|=\Phi(\sigma(A))$ for a symmetric gauge function $\Phi$ on $\mathbb R^{\min(m,n)}$, i.e. a norm invariant under permutations and sign changes. (Fan dominance) $\|A\|\le\|B\|$ for every orthogonally invariant norm iff $\|A\|_{(k)}\le\|B\|_{(k)}$ for every $k$, i.e. iff $\sigma(A)\prec_w\sigma(B)$.

**Hypotheses.**
- $\|\cdot\|$ a norm on $\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ with $\|UAV\|=\|A\|$ for orthogonal $U\in O(m)$, $V\in O(n)$
- For Fan dominance: $A,B\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$

**Conclusion.** $\|A\|=\Phi(\sigma_1(A),\dots,\sigma_{\min(m,n)}(A))$ with $\Phi$ a symmetric gauge function, and conversely every symmetric gauge function defines such a norm; $\big(\forall\,\|\cdot\|\ \text{UI}:\ \|A\|\le\|B\|\big)\iff\forall k,\ \|A\|_{(k)}\le\|B\|_{(k)}$

**Sources.** Bhatia (`bhatia97`) Thm IV.2.1 (von Neumann), Thm IV.2.2 (Fan dominance); Horn (`hj13`) §7.4.7–7.4.8

### `horn-product-inequality` — Horn's product inequality and weak majorization of σ(AB)

*bound* · area `matrix-majorization` (Majorization, Ky Fan and unitarily invariant norms) · difficulty 4 · priority `later` · **Assume.**

For $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times p}$, $B\in\mathbb R^{p\times n}$ and every $k$: $\prod_{i\le k}\sigma_i(AB)\le\prod_{i\le k}\sigma_i(A)\sigma_i(B)$ (Horn). Consequently (Weyl's majorant theorem) $\sum_{i\le k}\sigma_i(AB)^s\le\sum_{i\le k}\sigma_i(A)^s\sigma_i(B)^s$ for every $s>0$; in particular $\|AB\|_{(k)}\le\sum_{i\le k}\sigma_i(A)\sigma_i(B)$.

**Hypotheses.**
- $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times p}$, $B\in\mathbb R^{p\times n}$ (`Fin` indices)
- $1\le k\le\min(m,p,n)$, $s>0$

**Conclusion.** $\prod_{i\le k}\sigma_i(AB)\le\prod_{i\le k}\sigma_i(A)\sigma_i(B)$ and $\sum_{i\le k}\sigma_i(AB)^s\le\sum_{i\le k}\big(\sigma_i(A)\sigma_i(B)\big)^s$

**Sources.** Horn (`hj91`) Thm 3.3.4 (Horn), Thm 3.3.14 (weak majorization); Marshall (`mo11`) 9.H.1

**Mathlib has.** `ExteriorAlgebra / exteriorPower` (Exterior powers exist, but compound matrices and σ(Λᵏ A) = products of σᵢ do not.)

**Mathlib lacks.** Horn's inequality

**NLAlib builds on.** `NLAlib.singularValues_mul_le_mul_specNorm` (k = 1 type bound σᵢ(AC) ≤ σᵢ(A)‖C‖). Depends on: `majorization-def`, `svd`, `courant-fischer`.

**Consumers.** schatten-norms-def, nystrom-randomized / HMT nuclear-norm variants, rsvd-tail.

**Notes.** The standard proof applies the k = 1 case to compound matrices; the additive corollary needs the log-majorization ⇒ weak majorization lemma (majorization-def with φ = exp). Consumers: schatten-norms-def (product Hölder), Schatten/nuclear-norm bounds of products in low-rank approximation (e.g. ‖Σ₂Ω₂Ω₁†‖_* in HMT-type nuclear bounds, nystrom-randomized), rsvd-tail. Recommendation: assume.

### `kronecker-spectral` — Spectrum, singular values and norms of Kronecker products

*identity* · area `matrix-spectral` (SVD, eigenvalues and perturbation) · difficulty 2 · priority `later` · **Prove.**

If $A=U_A\Sigma_AV_A^\top$ and $B=U_B\Sigma_BV_B^\top$ then $A\otimes B=(U_A\otimes U_B)(\Sigma_A\otimes\Sigma_B)(V_A\otimes V_B)^\top$, so the singular values of $A\otimes B$ are $\{\sigma_i(A)\sigma_j(B)\}$; hence $\|A\otimes B\|=\|A\|\|B\|$, $\|A\otimes B\|_F=\|A\|_F\|B\|_F$, $\operatorname{rank}(A\otimes B)=\operatorname{rank}A\cdot\operatorname{rank}B$, $(A\otimes B)^\dagger=A^\dagger\otimes B^\dagger$. For symmetric $A,B$ the eigenvalues of $A\otimes B$ are $\lambda_i(A)\lambda_j(B)$ and those of $A\otimes I+I\otimes B$ are $\lambda_i(A)+\lambda_j(B)$.

**Hypotheses.**
- $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$, $B\in\mathbb R^{p\times q}$ (any `Fintype` indices; product index types)
- Eigenvalue statements: $A=A^\top$, $B=B^\top$

**Conclusion.** $\sigma(A\otimes B)=\{\sigma_i(A)\sigma_j(B)\}$ as multisets; $\|A\otimes B\|=\|A\|\|B\|$; $\|A\otimes B\|_F=\|A\|_F\|B\|_F$; $\operatorname{rank}(A\otimes B)=\operatorname{rank}A\operatorname{rank}B$; $(A\otimes B)^\dagger=A^\dagger\otimes B^\dagger$

**Sources.** Horn (`hj91`) Thm 4.2.12 (eigenvalues), Thm 4.2.15 (SVD); Loan (`vl00`) §2

**Mathlib has.** `Matrix.mul_kronecker_mul`; `Matrix.inv_kronecker`; `Matrix.det_kronecker`; `Matrix.trace_kronecker`; `Matrix.conjTranspose_kronecker`; `Matrix.PosSemidef.kronecker`; `Matrix.vec` (Column-stacking vectorisation (Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.Vec).); `Matrix.kronecker_mulVec_vec` (The vec trick vec(AXBᵀ) = (B ⊗ A) vec X.); `Matrix.vec_dotProduct_vec`

**Consumers.** Kronecker/tensor sketches (future sketching entries), gaussian-frob-second-moment, gaussian-amm.
